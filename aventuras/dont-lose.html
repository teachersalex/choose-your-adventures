Part 1

The Rules

Lisbon, 8:30 a.m. You are a tour guide from Brazil. Today is your first tour alone.

Your boss, Mrs. Costa, gives you a paper.

COSTA TOURS

Rule 1: Don’t lose anyone.

Rule 2: Be at the hotel at 7 p.m. The fado show is at 7:30.

Rule 3: Talk to the people who live there. They know everything.

“Today is your test,” she says. “If the day is good, the job is yours.”

In the van, Rui, the driver, says: “Bom dia!” Bob and Linda are from Texas. Today is their fortieth wedding anniversary. Yuki is from Japan. She has a big camera, and she wants one perfect photo. Helena is a quiet woman from London. She writes in a small notebook all the time.

The plan is Sintra. But Rui looks at his phone. “There is a lot of fog in Sintra today.”

“And the waves in Nazaré are big today!” Yuki says.

What do you do?

Part 2

Fog

Sintra is green and beautiful. On the top of a hill, there is a famous palace: Pena Palace. King Ferdinand II built it in the 1800s. It is red and yellow, like a palace from a fairy tale.

But today, you can’t see it. There is fog everywhere. White, white, white.

“Forty years of marriage, and I can’t see the palace,” Linda says. She is sad. Yuki puts her camera in her bag. Helena writes in her notebook.

Near a café, an old woman sells travesseiros, a famous sweet from Sintra. She hears Linda, and she laughs.

“Fog? Go down to Quinta da Regaleira,” she says. “There is a deep well there, under the ground. Down there, you don’t need the sun.”

Part 3

Óbidos

Rui drives north. One hour later, you are in Óbidos, a small white town inside old walls.

You tell the group the story of the town: “In 1282, King Dinis gave this town to Queen Isabel. It was a wedding present!”

Linda laughs. “Bob! Forty years, and I don’t have a town!”

A woman sells ginjinha, a sweet cherry drink, in small chocolate cups. First you drink, then you eat the cup. Everybody loves it.

“Now Nazaré!” Yuki says. “The big waves! My perfect photo!”

But the ginjinha woman shakes her head. “Nazaré is full of tourists today. Go to Peniche. It’s only twenty-five minutes from here. It has an old fortress by the sea and the best fish in Portugal.”

When Helena hears the word “Peniche,” she stops writing. She looks at you. But she says nothing.

What do you do?

Part 4

Waiting

You wait. Ten minutes. Thirty minutes. One hour. The fog stays.

It is cold. Bob looks at his watch. Linda is quiet. Helena writes, but she doesn’t smile.

Then Yuki stands up. “I don’t want to wait. I want to walk in the park. Maybe I can find a good photo in the fog.”

“Me too,” Bob says. “I want to walk.”

The park around the palace is very big. There are a lot of paths in the trees, and today you can’t see them well.

The paper with the rules is in your pocket. But everybody is bored, and everybody looks at you.

Part 5

The Well

Quinta da Regaleira is a strange and beautiful place. A very rich man built it more than one hundred years ago: Carvalho Monteiro. “He was born in Rio de Janeiro,” you tell the group. “In Brazil, like me!”

The famous well is not for water. It is like a tower, upside down, under the ground. You go down a stone staircase: nine levels, 27 meters.

Yuki points her camera up at the circle of sky. Click. “My perfect photo!” Helena smiles for the first time today.

At three o’clock, Bob holds Linda’s hand. “We got married on a beach, forty years ago. Can we see the sea today? At sunset?”

“Cabo da Roca is twenty minutes from here,” Rui says. “But the sun goes down at seven.” And at seven, you need to be at the hotel.

Part 6

Nazaré

Nazaré is a fishing town. On a cliff, there is an old fort with a small red lighthouse. From here, people watch the famous waves.

You tell the group: “Under the sea here, there is a giant canyon. It makes the waves very big. In 2020, a Brazilian woman, Maya Gabeira, surfed a wave of 22.4 meters here. A world record for women!”

The giant waves come in winter. Today, the waves are not giant, but they are big and loud.

Yuki is very excited. She sees some rocks near the water. “From there, I can get my perfect photo! Five minutes!” She wants to go down alone.

Next to the fort, a young man in a wetsuit looks at the sea. He is a surfer from Nazaré.

Part 7

Peniche

Peniche is a fishing town by the sea. Next to the water, there is a big old fortress.

You tell the group: “During the dictatorship, from 1934 to 1974, this fortress was a prison. The prisoners were people against the government. Today, it is a museum about freedom.”

Helena stops in front of the big door. Her hands shake. “Can we go in?” she asks. Her voice is very low.

But Bob and Linda want fish for lunch on the beach. And Yuki wants photos of the surfers. The plan is: beach, lunch, then Lisbon.

Part 8

The Office

You say no. And all day, you say no. You follow the plan, minute by minute.

Lunch at one. Cabo da Roca at three, for twenty minutes. Nobody smiles in the photos. At 6:30 p.m., you are at the hotel. Nobody is lost. You are early.

At the fado show, Bob sleeps. Linda looks at her phone. Yuki’s camera stays in her bag. Helena closes her notebook. “There is one place in Portugal I want to see,” she says. “But not on this tour.”

The next morning, Mrs. Costa reads the reviews.

★★☆☆☆ Correct.

★★☆☆☆ On time.

★☆☆☆☆ Boring.

“You followed Rule 1 and Rule 2,” she says. “But you didn’t talk to people. Rule 3, remember?”

Now you have a desk in the office. You answer emails all day. From your window, you see the van. Rui is in it, with a new guide.

    Part 9

    Where Is Yuki?

    At the meeting time, everybody is at the van. Everybody... but not Yuki.

    You call her. No answer. You look for her. One hour. Two hours. Many hours. Rui looks too. Bob and Linda wait in the van. They are cold and hungry.

    At 9 p.m., your phone rings. It’s the police. A man found Yuki, far from the van. She is OK, but she is cold and tired, and her phone has no battery.

    No hotel at seven. No fado show for Bob and Linda’s anniversary.

    In the van, Yuki doesn’t look at you. “You are the guide,” she says. “Why did you let me go alone?”

    Helena writes one line in her notebook. Then she closes it.

    The next morning, Mrs. Costa puts the paper with the rules on her table. Her finger is on Rule 1.

    “Don’t lose anyone,” she reads. “Goodbye.”

        Part 11

        Our Own Van

        Cabo da Roca is the westernmost point of continental Europe. The cliffs are 140 meters high. The Atlantic is blue and wild.

        On a stone monument, there are words from Camões, the great poet of Portugal: “Aqui, onde a terra se acaba e o mar começa.” You translate: “Here, where the land ends and the sea begins.”

        At seven, the sun goes down into the sea. Rui plays a fado on his phone. Bob and Linda dance. Yuki takes a hundred photos. Helena closes her eyes.

        You are very late. At 9 p.m., Mrs. Costa calls. She is very angry. “Rule 2! You are fired!”

        Rui looks at you. “Then I quit too,” he says. “I want to work with you.”

        One month later, there is a small blue van in Lisbon. On the door, there are two names: Rui’s and yours. The first review comes from Texas:

        ★★★★★ Bob and Linda: The best guide in Portugal!

          Part 12

          The Big Wave

          The surfer’s name is Tiago. “The rocks? No!” he says. “The sea takes people from there. Come with me.”

          Tiago takes the group to a safe place on the cliff. Below, two of his friends surf a big wave. Click! Yuki has her perfect photo.

          On the way back, you tell the group about the canyon, the fort and the surfers. Then a family from Brazil asks you a question, and you answer in Portuguese.

          Tiago listens. “English and Portuguese? Perfect,” he says. “In winter, people come from all over the world to see the giant waves. Our surf team needs a guide. Do you want the job?”

          At seven, everybody is at the hotel. Mrs. Costa is happy. But that night, you can’t sleep. You think about the waves.

          Winter comes. You stand at the red lighthouse with thirty tourists. A giant wave comes. Everybody screams. You smile. This is your job now.

            Part 13

            The Fortress

            You all go in. Near the entrance, there is a big metal memorial with more than 2,500 names of prisoners.

            Helena reads slowly. Then she stops. She touches one name.

            “My father,” she says. “He was here for eight years. I was a little girl. Every month, my mother and I came here to see him. We sat on one side of a room, and he sat on the other side.”

            Bob takes off his hat. Linda holds Helena’s hand. Yuki puts her camera down.

            “On April 27, 1974, the prisoners of Peniche were free. My father too. Later, I went to London, and I never came back. Until today. My father died last winter.” She opens her notebook. “Today, all day, I wrote to him about Portugal.”

            At 6:58 p.m., you are at the hotel. Two minutes early.

            Every year now, on April 27, Helena comes back to Peniche. And every year, she asks Costa Tours for one guide: you.
