Part 1

Friday, 7 p.m.

New York. Pearson Hardman, one of the best law firms in the city. This is your first week.

Louis Litt puts ten boxes on your desk. “Read everything. Monday morning, on my desk.”

Then Donna, Harvey’s assistant, stops at your desk. She knows everything. “Harvey wants you. Now.”

Harvey’s office has glass walls and old vinyl records on a shelf.

“Tomorrow at nine, my client buys twelve hotels. Two hundred million dollars. Something in this deal smells bad. Read the papers tonight and find the problem. And one rule: don’t lie to me. Ever.”

“And Louis’s boxes?”

“Louis is your problem. Not mine.”

What do you do?

Part 2

Page 300

The file room is cold. Harvey’s papers. Your fourth coffee. Page 100. Page 200. Page 300.

You stop.

Page 300 is a letter from the city to the seller. Two of the twelve hotels close in June. The buildings are not safe. The seller knows, but the contract doesn’t say a word about it.

At three a.m., the phone on the wall rings.

“My name is Nick Cole, the seller’s lawyer. That letter is in your papers by mistake. Bring it to me, and on Monday you work for my firm. Double the money. Your own office with a view. I’m in the diner across the street.”

What do you do?

Part 3

Louis’s Boxes

Box one. Box two. Box six. Louis’s papers are long and boring.

At one a.m., the office is empty. Only you and the boxes.

Then Donna walks in. Donna knows everything about everybody.

She puts a coffee on your desk. “Harvey never asks two times. This is your second chance. He doesn’t give a third.”

Part 4

Three A.M.

Harvey answers on the first ring. He doesn’t sleep.

You tell him about the letter. Two hotels. June. The seller knows.

Harvey is quiet for a moment. “Who else knows about this letter?”

“Nick Cole. He called me.”

“What did he offer you?”

The office with a view. Double the money. Harvey’s voice is cold. It’s your first week, and you are afraid.

Part 5

The Diner

The diner is almost empty. Nick Cole wears an expensive suit and a cold smile.

He puts a contract on the table. Your name is on it, with a number. A very big number.

“Sign here. Give me the letter. Go home and sleep. Monday, you start with us.”

You look at the number. Then you look at your bag. The letter is inside.

Part 6

Six A.M.

At six a.m., the office is dark. Only one light is on: Harvey’s.

Harvey sits at his desk with a coffee. Donna stands next to him. Of course.

“Donna says you went out at four,” Harvey says. “Where were you?”

The letter is in your hand. The big number is still in your head.

Part 7

Twenty More Boxes

Monday, nine a.m. You put the ten boxes on Louis’s desk. Louis doesn’t say thank you.

“Good. Here are twenty more.”

Through the glass, you see Harvey’s office. Harvey closed the hotel deal on Saturday, with another new lawyer. Now that lawyer sits in Harvey’s office, and Donna brings the coffee.

Harvey walks past your desk. He doesn’t look at you.

Louis smiles. “Don’t be sad. You work for me now. Forever.”

    Part 8

    The Other Side

    You sign. You give Nick the letter. He puts it in his jacket. “Welcome to the team.”

    Nine a.m. You sit next to Nick Cole. Across the table, Harvey Specter looks at you one time. Only one time.

    Then Harvey puts a paper on the table: a copy of the letter. “Page 300. Actually, I read it on Thursday.”

    Harvey’s client pays sixty million dollars less. Nick doesn’t look at you.

    On Monday, you have your office with a view. Nick puts a file on your desk. “Your first case. Against Pearson Hardman.”

    You open it. On page one: Harvey Specter. You are afraid.

      Part 9

      Harvey’s Team

      Nine a.m. The meeting room: Harvey, his client, Nick Cole and you.

      Nick smiles at Harvey. “Ask your new lawyer about my offer.”

      “A job at your firm,” Harvey says. “I know.”

      He puts the letter on the table. Nick doesn’t smile now. Harvey’s client pays sixty million dollars less.

      In the elevator, Harvey looks at you. “Actually, I read that letter on Thursday.”

      “Then why...?”

      “I knew the letter. I didn’t know you. Now I know you.” The doors open. “Monday, you work for me.”

      At her desk, Donna has a coffee for you.

        Part 10

        The Elevator

        Nine a.m. The meeting room: Harvey, his client, Nick Cole and you.

        Nick smiles at Harvey. “Ask your new lawyer about my offer.”

        Harvey doesn’t answer. He looks at you. Only at you.

        Then he puts the letter on the table, and his client pays sixty million dollars less.

        At eleven, you are in Jessica Pearson’s office. “You talked to the other side in the middle of a deal,” Jessica says. “Then you lied to Harvey. You don’t work here anymore.”

        At the door, Harvey says one thing: “I knew the letter. I didn’t know you. Now I know you.”

        You carry one small box to the elevator.
