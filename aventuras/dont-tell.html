Part 1

Halloween

It’s Halloween at Hogwarts. In the Great Hall, there are pumpkins with candles everywhere, and real bats fly over the tables. Everybody is happy. You are not. Your friend Milo isn’t here. Yesterday, after class, he went to the library. Nobody saw him after that.

Nearly Headless Nick, the Gryffindor ghost, floats down next to you. “I know where your friend is,” he says quietly. “On the seventh floor, there’s an old painting of a boy in a green coat. The boy collects names. If you tell him your name, you go into his painting. Six students are in there now. Your friend is number six. With a seventh name, the boy can walk out of the painting. And he takes the face of student number seven.”

“What’s his name?” you ask.

“I knew him in 1891,” Nick says. “But everybody who knew him forgot his name. That’s his magic. Say his real name to the painting, and the magic breaks. Old things remember it: the trophies, the old books. But hurry. At midnight, your friend stays in the painting forever. And never, never tell him your name.”

At the next table, Rowan Pike, a big second-year, laughs. “A painting that eats first-years? I want to see that.” Rowan loves his own name. He tells it to everybody.

What do you do?

Part 2

The Boy in the Green Coat

The corridor on the seventh floor is short and dark. Nobody comes here. At the end of it, there’s a big painting in a gold frame. A small gold plate under it says: THE BOY IN THE GREEN COAT.

The boy in the painting is about sixteen, with black hair and a perfect smile. Behind him, in the dark, there are six small faces. The last face is Milo’s. He looks pale and very afraid. In the corner of the painting, there are two small letters: S.C.

“Ah, Milo’s friend,” the boy says. “He talks about you all the time. Listen. I don’t need Milo. He cries all day. Tell me your name, and he walks out. Your name for his. Easy.”

Milo’s mouth moves, but you can’t hear him.

You want to help your friend more than anything.

Part 3

Milo’s Notebook

The library is empty. Everybody is at the feast. Under a table at the back, you find Milo’s bag, and his notebook is inside. Milo is Muggle-born. Everything at Hogwarts is new to him, so he writes everything down.

On the last page, in Milo’s small handwriting:

THE BOY IN THE GREEN COAT. 7th floor. Painted in 1891.

Nobody remembers his name. WHY???

The Book of Names, Restricted Section. Maybe his name is in it?

Tonight: go to the painting and ask him his name!

Poor Milo. He asked the boy his name. And then, of course, the boy asked Milo his name. Milo is a very polite boy.

You are angry and very worried. At the back of the library, there’s a rope. Behind it is the Restricted Section. Students can’t go in there without a note from a teacher.

Part 4

If You’re Brave

You turn and walk away. Behind you, the boy laughs.

“You want my name? Go and find it, then. It’s on my grave, in the Forbidden Forest, under the oldest tree. Go and read it, if you’re brave.”

You are angry, and you are afraid. At the top of the stairs, you stop. It’s ten o’clock. You have two hours.

Students can’t go into the Forbidden Forest. Everybody knows that. But Nick said old things remember, and a grave is an old thing. The Trophy Room is full of old things, too. And it’s inside the castle.

Part 5

The Trophy Room

The Trophy Room is full of old silver cups, shields and medals. They shine in the light of the torches. You read name after name, cup after cup.

Then, in a dusty corner at the back, you find a small gold cup:

SILAS CRANE

Special Award for Services to the School, 1891

S.C. Silas Crane. It’s him! You read the name again and again. Nick knew Silas, so Nick forgot. But you never knew him, so you can remember.

Somewhere in the castle, a clock rings once. It’s half past eleven. The seventh floor is far, and you are only a first-year. Maybe this is a job for a teacher. But midnight is very close.

Part 6

The Forbidden Forest

Hagrid’s hut is dark. Hagrid is still at the feast. You walk past it and into the trees.

Inside the forest, it’s very cold and very quiet. You walk for a long time. There’s no old tree with a grave. There are only trees, and more trees.

Then you see spider webs. They are everywhere, and they are very big. Some are bigger than a door. Somewhere in the dark, something makes a noise. Click. Click-click.

You are very afraid. But the boy said the grave is here, and midnight is close. Maybe the grave is just a little further.

Part 7

The Restricted Section

You go under the rope. The books here are very old. Some of them whisper. One book screams when you touch it, and you put it back fast.

Then you find it: a small black book, cold as ice. On the first page, in old brown ink:

This book belongs to Silas Crane, 1891.

Silas Crane. 1891. The boy in the green coat. You know his name now!

Only two pages in the book have words on them:

TO TAKE A NAME: Write the name on this page. Give the page to my painting.

TO HIDE A NAME: Write your name on this page. Burn the page. Then nobody can take your name. Nobody can find it, ever again.

There’s a candle on the table. With a hidden name, you can go to the painting, and the boy can never take you.

What do you do?

Part 8

Professor Snape

You run out of the library with the black book under your robes. On the stairs, a tall man in black is waiting for you. Professor Snape.

“Out of your common room at eleven o’clock,” he says slowly. “With a book from the Restricted Section.” He holds out his hand. “Give it to me. And tell me what you are doing.”

Snape is not a nice man. But he’s a teacher, and Professor Dumbledore is in London tonight. Maybe Snape can help.

Behind you, a staircase starts to move. Hogwarts stairs do that. If you jump now, you can get away from him.

Part 9

Midnight

You run up the stairs, all the way to the seventh floor. From a dark corner, Mrs Norris watches you. You don’t stop.

The boy in the green coat isn’t smiling now. Behind him, Milo is very pale. He doesn’t move.

“So you know my name,” the boy says. “I can see it in your face. Listen to me. If you say it, the painting breaks, with everybody inside. Your friend, too. But tell me your name, and Milo walks out. Your name for his.”

Somewhere in the castle, a clock starts to ring. One. Two. Three.

You look at Milo. You remember Nick’s words, and the boy’s perfect smile.

Part 10

His Own Book

Snape shouts something, but the moving stairs carry you away from him.

On the seventh floor, at the end of a short, dark corridor, you find the painting: a boy in a green coat, in a gold frame. Behind him, there are six small faces. The last one is Milo. He’s very pale. It’s almost midnight.

The boy sees the black book in your hands. For the first time, he looks afraid.

“My book,” he says. “Please, don’t say my name. If you say it, the painting breaks, with everybody inside. Your friend, too. Let’s be friends. Use the book. Write any name on my page, and give it to me. A name for a name, and your friend walks out.” He smiles. “What about Rowan Pike, that big boy? He laughs at your friend. Paintings hear everything.”

You look at Milo. Then you look at the page.

What do you do?

Part 11

Your Name in Gold

“Your name is Silas Crane,” you say.

The boy screams. The gold frame breaks from top to bottom. Six students fall out of the painting onto the cold floor: a girl in a long old dress, a tall boy in a funny old hat, three more students in old clothes, and Milo.

Then the painting turns into grey dust. All over the castle, everything with Silas Crane’s name on it turns into dust, too.

Milo hugs you. He’s warm. He’s real. You are so happy, you laugh and cry at the same time.

The girl in the old dress looks around. “Excuse me,” she says. “What year is it?”

The next morning, in the Great Hall, Professor Dumbledore gives you a Special Award for Services to the School. Everybody claps. Even Professor Snape claps. Twice.

That afternoon, you and Milo go to the Trophy Room. There’s a new gold cup there. On it, there’s your name.

“Next time a painting asks my name,” Milo says, “I’m going to say ‘Rowan Pike.’”

    Part 12

    The Seventh Name

    You say your name.

    The boy smiles. “Thank you.”

    Suddenly, you are very cold. Everything is flat and full of colour. You can’t move your feet. You are inside the painting.

    The boy steps out of the frame and into the corridor. He has your face, your robes and your voice. He looks back at you and smiles. It’s your smile.

    “And Milo?” you shout. “You promised!”

    “Milo stays,” he says. “Now he has a friend.”

    The next day, he goes to your classes. He sits in your chair. He writes letters to your family. Even Professor Snape gives him ten points. Nobody notices.

    Under the frame, the gold plate is different now. It doesn’t say THE BOY IN THE GREEN COAT anymore. It says your name.

      Part 13

      The Collector

      You write ROWAN PIKE on the page, and you give it to the painting.

      Somewhere in the castle, Rowan Pike’s bed is suddenly empty. In the painting, there’s a new face: a big boy, very angry. He hits the inside of the frame with his hands. And Milo falls out of the painting onto the floor, warm and real.

      “How...?” he asks.

      “Magic,” you say.

      You keep the black book. You tell nobody. It was easy, very easy. And it felt good.

      Sometimes, late at night, you go up to the seventh floor. Silas Crane smiles at you. “You’re better at this than me,” he says.

      Ten years later, the Daily Prophet has a new story every month. Someone disappears, and a new face appears in an old painting. The Ministry doesn’t know who is doing it. People call this person the Collector.

      Only one person knows: Milo. And Milo never says your name.

        Part 14

        Eleven Forever

        You go deeper into the forest. There’s no old tree with a grave. There’s no grave. There never was. The boy lied.

        The clicking is all around you now. Then you see eyes in the dark. Eight eyes. Then many, many eyes.

        Then everything is dark.

        When you open your eyes again, the forest is quiet. You feel very cold and very light. You can see the trees through your hands.

        Nearly Headless Nick floats down to you. He looks very sad. “Oh dear,” he says. “Welcome.”

        You are a ghost.

        “You were afraid to die, so you stayed,” Nick says. “A lot of us are like that.” Then he smiles a little. “And on Halloween! I died on Halloween, too. Next year, we can have our deathday parties together.”

        Then he asks your name, and you tell him. It’s safe now. Nobody can take it.

        Every night, you float up to the seventh floor and talk to Milo through the frame. He’s eleven forever. So are you.

          Part 15

          No Name

          You write your name on the page and burn it in the candle. For a second, you feel very light and a little strange.

          Then you run to the seventh floor, to the painting of the boy in the green coat. Behind him, there are six small faces. One of them is Milo.

          “Hello,” the boy says, with a perfect smile. “What’s your name?”

          You open your mouth. Nothing comes out. You can’t remember it. You know who you are, but you don’t have a name anymore.

          The boy stops smiling. He can’t take a name that isn’t there.

          “Your name is Silas Crane,” you say.

          The boy screams, and the painting breaks. Six students fall out onto the floor. Milo hugs you. “Thank you! Thank you!” Then he looks at your face, confused. “Sorry... who are you?”

          The next day, the whole school talks about a brave student on the seventh floor. Nobody knows who.

          In the Trophy Room, there’s a new gold cup: Special Award for Services to the School. There’s no name on it. Only you know it’s yours.

          Every morning, Milo says hi to you. “Hi! Are you new?”

            Part 16

            Good Morning

            The teacher listens to you. Then: “A painting that collects students? That’s an old ghost story. Go to bed.”

            The teacher walks you to your common room and waits at the door. You go to bed, but you don’t sleep. Then, suddenly, it’s morning.

            At breakfast, Milo’s chair is empty. It’s empty forever now.

            At the next table, Rowan Pike is different today. He says please and thank you. He reads a book. He smiles a perfect smile.

            Last night, after you went to bed, Rowan went to see the painting. Of course he told it his name. Rowan tells everybody his name. This isn’t Rowan.

            He stands up and walks to your table. He puts his face very close to yours. “Good morning,” he says, and he says your name. “Thank you for staying in bed.”

            You never told him your name.
