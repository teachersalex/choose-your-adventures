Part 1

The Offer

You finished law school last month. Today is your first day at Hale & Price, one of the biggest law firms in the world. On the wall, in big gold letters: WE NEVER LOSE.

In the lobby, an old woman with a coffee cart gives you a coffee. Her name tag says BETH. “First day?” she says. “A little advice: don’t sign anything before you read it.”

On the 99th floor, Mr. Victor Price is waiting. He’s tall and elegant, with a perfect smile. He offers you a big salary, an apartment, and one day, your name on the door.

Then he gives you a gold pen and two papers. The first is your contract, forty pages long. The second is for Mrs. Rosa Lima, a little old lady with a chocolate shop.

“Two signatures,” he says. “Yours today. Hers tomorrow.”

What do you do?

Part 2

The Gold Pen

You sign. Mr. Price takes your contract and locks it in his desk. “Welcome to the family.”

Then he tells you about the case. Sweet Empire, the biggest candy company in America, sells Golden Bites: little chocolate balls in a gold box. Sweet Empire says it invented them in 1999. But Mrs. Lima, a Brazilian grandmother in Queens, sells the same chocolate balls in her shop. She calls them brigadeiros.

“Her paper is simple,” Mr. Price says. “She gets ten thousand dollars, and she never sells chocolate balls again. She’s eighty. She’s alone. Tomorrow, she signs.”

It sounds easy. But you know almost nothing about this case.

What do you do?

Part 3

Forty Pages

You don’t sign today. Mr. Price gives you until Friday.

That night, at home, you read all forty pages. Most of it is boring. But three clauses are very strange, and the last one is at the bottom of page 40, in very small letters:

Clause 9: The lawyer never goes home before midnight.

Clause 22: The lawyer never says no to Mr. Price.

Clause 40: The lawyer can never leave Hale & Price.

Then you read Mrs. Lima’s paper. It’s only one page. She gets ten thousand dollars, and she never sells her chocolate balls again. Sweet Empire, the biggest candy company in America, gets her recipe.

You are afraid of this firm. But if you leave, Mrs. Lima is alone against the biggest law firm in the world.

What do you do?

Part 4

The War Room

The war room is on the 98th floor. There are forty lawyers, three hundred boxes, and a lot of coffee. On a big screen, there’s a photo of one little chocolate ball. Everybody looks at it very seriously.

“This is a war,” Mr. Price says. “Sweet Empire invented Golden Bites in 1999. Mrs. Lima sells the same thing and calls it a brigadeiro. She signs her paper, or she loses everything.” He looks at you. “Go and bring me her signature.”

At the back of the room, there’s an old door that says ARCHIVE. A hundred years of old cases are down there. Maybe there’s a case like this one. But Mr. Price wants that signature today.

What do you do?

Part 5

Rosa’s Shop

Mrs. Lima’s shop is very small, and it smells of chocolate. On the window, in pink letters: ROSA’S BRAZILIAN SWEETS, SINCE 1985.

Mrs. Lima is eighty, very short, with big glasses and a big smile. She gives you a brigadeiro. “Eat, meu bem. You’re too thin.” The brigadeiro is perfect.

On the wall, there’s an old card in a frame. It’s in Portuguese:

Brigadeiro

Receita da mamãe, 1946

You give Mrs. Lima her paper. She looks at it for a long time. “My English is not very good,” she says. Then she takes your gold pen. “Where do I sign?”

She trusts you. And she can’t read the paper.

What do you do?

Part 6

The Archive

The archive is cold and quiet. Beth, the woman with the coffee cart, is there. She’s reading an old book.

“Nobody comes down here,” she says. “What are you looking for?”

You tell her about the case. “An old case about chocolate? No,” she says. “But read this.” She opens a very old box and gives you one page:

Rule 1: A lawyer of this firm never lies to a judge.

Rule 2: A lawyer of this firm never asks another lawyer to lie.

A lawyer who breaks these rules leaves the firm. Even the boss.

Elizabeth Hale, 1926

“Elizabeth Hale started this firm,” Beth says. “She was a great lawyer. Nobody reads her rules now.” She smiles. “I like big cases. Maybe I can come and watch yours.”

Maybe Mr. Price doesn’t know these rules. Or maybe he knows, and he doesn’t care.

What do you do?

Part 7

Old Words

Mr. Price reads the page. Then he laughs.

“Old words from an old woman,” he says, and he puts the page in his pocket. “This is a new firm. Here, we never lose.”

Then he smiles at you. “I like you. Bring me Mrs. Lima’s signature today, and you are a partner. The youngest partner in the history of Hale & Price. Your name on the door.”

He offers you his hand. You are angry about the page. But a partner, at your age!

What do you do?

Part 8

Plan B

You read the paper to Mrs. Lima in Portuguese. She listens. Then she gives you back the gold pen.

“No,” she says. “My mother’s recipe is not for sale.”

That night, Mr. Price calls you to his office. He isn’t smiling.

“Tomorrow, we go to court,” he says. “The judge is going to ask about her old card. You say it’s fake. Our expert says the paper is new.”

“Is it new?” you ask.

“No,” he says. “But the judge doesn’t know that.” Then he smiles again. “Win this, and you’re a partner. Your name on the door.”

You are afraid of tomorrow.

What do you do?

Part 9

Page 40

“I quit,” you tell Mr. Price.

He laughs. He takes your contract out of his desk, opens it to the last page, and points at the very small letters at the bottom:

Hale & Price, contract, page 40

Clause 40: The lawyer can never leave Hale & Price.

“You signed it,” he says. “You didn’t read it. That’s your problem, not mine. Tomorrow, you tell the judge the card is fake. That’s all.”

You are angry with Mr. Price. And you are angry with yourself.

What do you do?

Part 10

The Court

The next morning, the court is full. Mrs. Lima sits in the front row with her old card and a box of brigadeiros. Behind her, people from her street hold small posters: WE LOVE ROSA.

Mr. Price sits next to you.

The judge, a serious man with white hair, looks at the card for a long time. Then he looks at you.

“Counselor,” he says, “is this card real, or is it fake? I want the truth.”

Mr. Price whispers: “Say it’s fake.”

Everybody is looking at you. Mrs. Lima, too.

What do you do?

Part 11

Your Name on the Door

You do what Mr. Price wants. It’s easy. It’s very easy.

Hale & Price wins the case, of course. Sweet Empire sells Golden Bites in every store in America, and Mrs. Lima’s shop closes.

Mr. Price is very happy with you. You get a big office, then a bigger one. You never go home before midnight. You never lose. And you never say no to Mr. Price.

Ten years later, your name is on the door, in gold.

Today, a young lawyer sits in your office. It’s her first day, and she is nervous. You give her a contract and a gold pen.

“Sign here,” you say. And you smile, a perfect smile.

      The First Loss

        Part 14

        The Old Rules

        You stand up and give the judge the page from the archive.

        “Rule 2, Your Honor,” you say. “A lawyer of this firm never asks another lawyer to lie. Mr. Price wants me to say the card is fake. It’s real.”

        Mr. Price laughs. “That page is a hundred years old.”

        At the back of the court, someone stands up. It’s Beth, in her coffee apron. “It’s still the rule,” she says. “My name is Elizabeth Hale. My grandmother started this firm, and half of it is mine.” She looks at Mr. Price. “Victor, you know the rules. Go.”

        Mr. Price walks out. The judge eats a brigadeiro and closes the case. Mrs. Lima wins.

        That afternoon, Beth gives you a new contract and a pen. “I want you as my partner,” she says. “Your name on the door, next to my grandmother’s. Sign here.”

        You smile. “First, I read it.”
